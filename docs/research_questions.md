# Research questions

The project asks four linked questions. Each is broken into sub-questions, and each sub-question says what would count as an answer, so that the theory phase produces claims the empirical phase can test.

---

## Q1. How do countries grow and develop?

| # | Sub-question | What would count as an answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What share of cross-country income gaps is factor accumulation vs. productivity (TFP)? | Development-accounting decomposition; LAC vs. comparators. |
| 1.2 | Is productivity growth mainly *within* sectors/firms or *between* them (reallocation)? | Shift-share decompositions at sector, segment (formal/informal, size class) and firm level. |
| 1.3 | Through what mechanisms does productivity rise: technology adoption, capability accumulation, reallocation, scale, learning? | A mechanism map with evidence for each, and which dominate at which income level. |
| 1.4 | Why do some growth accelerations persist and others fade? | Characterization of sustained vs. failed episodes in terms of structure and capabilities. |

## Q2. What types of jobs, sectors and economic structure are needed?

| # | Sub-question | What would count as an answer |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Is manufacturing still special, and if so, which of its properties made it special? | Decomposition of the "manufacturing bundle" into separable properties. |
| 2.2 | Can services (or resource-based activities) substitute as an escalator? Which ones? | Activities scored on the same properties as manufacturing. |
| 2.3 | Does the *firm* and *job* structure (size distribution, formality, internal ladders) matter more than the *sector* structure? | Evidence on whether within-sector dualism explains more of the gap than sector mix. |
| 2.4 | What sequence or combination of activities sustains growth? | Theoretical conditions under which an economy keeps both absorbing labor and raising productivity. |

## Q3. Are some jobs better than others?

| # | Sub-question | What would count as an answer |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Better in what sense? Productivity level, productivity growth, learning for the worker, spillovers to others, labor absorption, stability. | An explicit, measurable definition of a job's *developmental value* (see `docs/theory/01_framework.md`). |
| 3.2 | Do workers in some jobs accumulate more human capital (steeper experience-wage profiles)? | Experience profiles by sector × firm size × formality. |
| 3.3 | Are the private and social values of jobs different (externalities), so that markets under-supply good jobs? | Identification of the wedge and its sources. |

## Q4. Why does LAC grow so slowly?

| # | Sub-question | What would count as an answer |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | What are the stylized facts? (TFP stagnation, growth-reducing structural change, premature deindustrialization, informality, firm-size distribution, low complexity, commodity dependence, volatility.) | A documented, sourced fact base. |
| 4.2 | Which candidate explanations are consistent with *all* the facts? | Confronting each theory with the fact base; a ranked diagnosis. |
| 4.3 | Why does the low-productivity state persist (what makes it an equilibrium)? | Explicit feedback loops / complementarities that make it self-reinforcing. |
| 4.4 | How does LAC differ from comparators (East Asia, Emerging Europe, resource-rich high-income countries)? | Comparative diagnosis. |
| 4.5 | What does this imply for policy (productive development, competition, labor/social insurance, macro)? | Policy implications that follow from the diagnosis, with the evidence each would need. |

---

## Scope (see decision log D-002, D-003)

- **Geography:** LAC as a whole, with emphasis on the larger economies (Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru) and, where data allow, Central America and the Caribbean. Comparators: East Asia, Emerging Europe, resource-rich advanced economies (Australia, Canada, Norway, New Zealand).
- **Period:** Long run (1950 onward) for stylized facts; 1990 onward for detailed structural and firm-level analysis.
- **Unit of analysis:** Deliberately multi-level — sector, segment (formal/informal × size class), firm, and job/worker.
