# Strand 6 — Are some jobs better than others?

Register IDs: L008, L048–L051, L054, L055.

## Three senses in which a job can be "better"

1. **For the worker, now:** higher wages, stability, benefits, safety (the labor-economics/ILO "decent work" sense).
2. **For the worker, over time:** a job that builds human capital, so earnings grow with experience. **Lagakos et al. (L048):** experience-wage profiles are about twice as steep in rich as in poor countries; education explains only about a third. Jobs in poor countries teach less — or workers can't move to where their learning pays.
3. **For others (externalities):** jobs that generate spillovers — knowledge diffusion, supplier upgrading, management training of future entrepreneurs, social cohesion. **Rodrik & Sabel (L050)** emphasize social externalities of scarce good jobs; **Lucas (L008)** and the capabilities literature emphasize knowledge externalities.

## Evidence that the distinction is binding in developing economies

- **Africa's manufacturing puzzle (Diao, Ellis, McMillan & Rodrik L051):** large firms raise productivity but hire few; small firms hire but don't improve. The economy has jobs that are productive *or* jobs that are plentiful, not both.
- **Services (Rodrik & Sandhu L049):** most new urban jobs are in low-productivity services; making them better jobs requires upgrading the firms and the technologies they use.
- **New technologies (Rodrik L055; ILO-World Bank L054):** current technologies are skill- and capital-biased. In LAC, 26–38% of jobs are exposed to generative AI, with augmentation gains concentrated among formal, urban, educated workers and up to half of potential gains blocked by the digital divide. Without deliberate design, new technology widens the gap between good and bad jobs.

## What this strand gives the project

A definition of "good job" that is useful for **growth** (not only welfare) must include: productivity level, productivity *growth* potential, learning for the worker, spillovers, and — crucially — the job's **scalability** (can it be replicated for many workers without its productivity collapsing?). This is formalized as the *developmental value of a job* in the framework.

## Gaps

- No standard metric of job-level learning; experience profiles by firm type are rare outside a few countries (Brazil's RAIS, Mexico's IMSS, Chile's administrative data could provide them).
- The externality wedge between private and social value of good jobs is asserted more than estimated.
