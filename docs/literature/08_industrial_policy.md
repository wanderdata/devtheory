# Strand 8 — Industrial and productive development policy

Register IDs: L014, L046, L049, L050, L052.

- **New economics of industrial policy (Juhász, Lane & Rodrik L052):** new causal studies (measurement + identification + structure) are more favorable than the old correlational work; effects are context-specific; tools go well beyond subsidies (public inputs, coordination, demand, procurement); governance and state capacity are central; deindustrialization reshapes what industrial policy should target.
- **Self-discovery and coordination (Hausmann & Rodrik L014):** market failures in discovering new activities and coordinating complementary investments.
- **Capabilities escalator (Cirera & Maloney L046):** sequence support to match firm and state capabilities.
- **Services-oriented policy (Rodrik & Sandhu L049) and good-jobs governance (Rodrik & Sabel L050):** iterative public–private collaboration aimed at employment quality, not only output.
- **LAC history:** import substitution (1950–80) delivered growth and industrialization but with high-cost, inward-oriented firms; the 1990s reforms removed many distortions but did not deliver productivity growth (Kehoe & Ruhl L063). Productive development policies (IDB, 2014 "Rethinking Productive Development" — to add to register) re-legitimized targeted public inputs.

## Relevance

Phase 1 is theory, so policy is downstream. The framework should generate policy implications only where they follow from a mechanism, and should say what evidence would distinguish them.
