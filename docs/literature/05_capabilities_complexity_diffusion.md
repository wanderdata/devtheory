# Strand 5 — Capabilities, complexity and technology diffusion

Register IDs: L014–L017, L045–L047, L053, L071.

## Capabilities and what you produce

- **Self-discovery (Hausmann & Rodrik L014):** discovering profitable new activities is costly and the knowledge spills over to imitators, so markets under-invest in experimentation.
- **What you export matters (Hausmann, Hwang & Rodrik L015):** export baskets associated with richer countries predict faster subsequent growth.
- **Complexity and the product space (Hidalgo & Hausmann L016; Hidalgo et al. L017):** a country's capabilities are revealed by the diversity and ubiquity of its exports; diversification proceeds to "nearby" products. Resource-based specializations are peripheral, making jumps hard.
- **Causal evidence (Atkin, Costinot & Fukui L053):** a country's capability growth rises with the average complexity of its industry mix (identified via other countries' WTO accessions). What you produce affects how fast your capabilities grow.

## Diffusion: extensive vs. intensive margins

- **Comin & Mestieri (L045):** adoption *lags* across countries have converged — technologies arrive everywhere — but *penetration* (intensity of use) has diverged; diffusion patterns account for about 80% of income divergence since 1820. This is the macro counterpart of LAC's within-firm stagnation: technologies are present, but used narrowly and shallowly.

## Management and organizational capabilities

- **Management practices explain productivity (Bloom & Van Reenen L047); low-productivity firms in developing countries reflect poor management, weak competition, and limited delegation (Bloom et al. L071).**
- **Innovation paradox (Cirera & Maloney L046):** returns to innovation should be high in developing countries, yet firms invest little because complementary capabilities (management, skills, state capacity) are missing. Build managerial practices first; sequence support along a "capabilities escalator."

## What this strand gives the project

1. A dynamic reason some activities are better: they build capabilities that compound (Lucas; Atkin et al.).
2. A precise reading of LAC's TFP problem as an **intensive-margin diffusion failure** — technology is available but organizations do not absorb it deeply.
3. A link to firm structure: organizational capabilities live in firms with internal hierarchies; a workforce in micro-units has little access to them.

## Gaps

- Complexity measures use exports and miss non-tradable services, where most LAC workers are.
- Capabilities are usually measured at the country level; the *worker-level* channel (people learning inside organizations) is under-developed — taken up in strand 6.
