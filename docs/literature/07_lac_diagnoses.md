# Strand 7 — Why LAC grows slowly: existing diagnoses

Register IDs: L004, L011, L012, L032, L038–L042, L057–L063, L069.

The literature offers several families of explanation. They are often presented as rivals; the project treats them as layers (see framework, Idea 4).

## A. Structuralist and neo-structuralist (ECLAC)

- **Prebisch (L004):** center–periphery; primary exporters face weak terms of trade and absorb technology slowly → industrialize.
- **Fajnzylber (L062):** the "empty box" — almost no LAC country combined dynamic growth with equity; requires endogenous technical progress, not "spurious" competitiveness from low wages or depreciation.
- **Ocampo (L061):** growth depends on the capacity to generate new dynamic activities and domestic linkages.
- **Current ECLAC framing (L042):** a prolonged low-growth period (~2.2% in 2025, ~2.3% projected for 2026) and "traps" of low growth and high inequality.

## B. Productivity / misallocation / competition (IDB, World Bank, IMF, neoclassical)

- **It's TFP (Cole, Ohanian, Riascos & Schmitz L038; Daude & Fernández-Arias L069; Restuccia L039):** LAC is the only poor Western group not converging; the gap is TFP, not human capital; competitive barriers (trade and domestic entry) are a plausible cause.
- **The Age of Productivity (Pagés L032):** low productivity concentrated in services and small firms.
- **Misallocation and informality (Busso et al. L031; Levy L033).**
- **Competition (World Bank 2024, L040):** LAC growth forecasts lowest of all regions; high markups; concentrated markets with a few large firms and many tiny ones; ~70% of workers self-employed or in firms <10 workers.
- **IMF 2025 (L041):** LA5 TFP ~52% of US; TFP growth negative (~−0.7%/yr, 2000–19); weakness is within surviving firms; sector mix does not explain it; frictions = size-based policies, finance, competition.

## C. Natural resources and macro volatility

- **Resource curse (Sachs & Warner L058) vs. "neither curse nor destiny" (Lederman & Maloney L057):** outcomes depend on institutions and knowledge capabilities built around resources (cf. Australia, Canada, Norway, and Chile's partial success).
- **Real exchange rate (Rodrik L060):** undervaluation helps tradables, which suffer more from market/institutional failures; commodity booms do the opposite.
- **Volatility (Ramey & Ramey L059):** LAC's commodity cycles, sudden stops, and inflation/debt crises lower growth.

## D. Institutions and political economy

- **Colonial and endowment-driven inequality (AJR L011; Engerman & Sokoloff L012):** extractive institutions and inequality limited broad access to schooling, land and political voice.
- **Reforms without growth (Kehoe & Ruhl L063):** Mexico liberalized but did not grow; candidate frictions: finance, contract enforcement, labor markets.

## Assessment

Each family explains part of the fact base and fails elsewhere:
- Structuralism explains the absorption problem and commodity dependence, but not why manufacturing-strong Mexico also stagnated.
- Misallocation explains within-sector stagnation, but is mostly static and silent on why distortions persist.
- Resource/macro explains cycles and RER-driven deindustrialization, but not the trend stagnation in non-commodity economies (Central America, Mexico).
- Institutions explain persistence, but are slow-moving and cannot explain timing (e.g., why the 1950–80 period was faster).

The framework's Idea 4 proposes a layered, self-reinforcing equilibrium that integrates these.
