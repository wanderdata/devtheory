## 2. Tensions in the literature

**T1 — Sector vs. firm.** Sector-level work (F4) says LAC's problem is *where labor goes*; firm-level work (F9) says *industry mix doesn't matter*, it's within-firm productivity. Both can be true if the relevant boundary is a **segment** boundary (formal/large vs. informal/micro) running through every industry. Labor flowed from modern segments (manufacturing, formal firms) into traditional segments (informal services) — growth-reducing at the sector level, invisible to an industry-mix counterfactual on formal-firm data. → Framework Idea 1.

**T2 — Is manufacturing special?** Kaldor/Rodrik say yes; services-led work says some services can substitute. Resolution: what was special was a *bundle* of properties, and that bundle has come apart. → Idea 2.

**T3 — Static vs. dynamic losses.** Misallocation estimates are static. Life-cycle wage evidence and capability-complexity evidence point to large *dynamic* losses from where people work. → Idea 3.

**T4 — Rival LAC diagnoses.** Structuralist, misallocation, resource/macro, and institutional accounts each explain some facts but not others (see strand 7). → Idea 4 integrates them as mutually reinforcing layers.

**T5 — Can within-segment upgrading absorb labor?** Upgrading non-tradable services raises productivity but, with inelastic demand, displaces workers. Absorption needs either income growth or tradability. → Idea 5.

## 3. Gaps the project can fill

1. A unit of analysis that unifies structural change and misallocation (segments).
2. A measurable definition of a job's *developmental value* that goes beyond wage level.
3. An integration of dynamic (learning, capability) channels into the LAC diagnosis.
4. An explicit equilibrium account of why LAC's low-productivity structure persists.
5. A treatment of the demand/absorption constraint on services-led growth.
