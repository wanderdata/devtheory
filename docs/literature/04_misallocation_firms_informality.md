# Strand 4 — Misallocation, firm dynamics and informality

Register IDs: L028–L031, L033–L037, L064, and L041 (IMF 2025).

## Misallocation

- **Hsieh & Klenow (L028):** dispersion in marginal revenue products implies large TFP gains from reallocation. **Restuccia & Rogerson (L030):** distortions correlated with productivity (taxing good firms, subsidizing bad ones) are especially damaging.
- **LAC magnitudes (Busso, Madrigal & Pagés L031):** LAC TFP has not risen since the mid-1970s; dispersion and misallocation are much larger than in the US; efficient reallocation would raise manufacturing TFP by roughly 45–127%.
- **Recent update (IMF 2025, L041):** misallocation costs Latin America around 16% of TFP relative to advanced economies (larger in full-economy estimates); frictions bear hardest on high-productivity firms.

## Firm life cycles

- **Flat life cycles (Hsieh & Klenow L029):** surviving plants in Mexico (and India) grow far less with age than in the US.
- **Colombia (Eslava, Haltiwanger & Pinzón L034):** slower life-cycle growth driven by a thin upper tail of high-growth young plants and weak selection ("up or out" is muted); ~87% of establishments are micro; only ~8% of plants born small become medium or large.
- **Survivors' within-firm stagnation (IMF 2025, L041):** for LA5 in 2000–19, within-firm productivity of surviving firms *subtracted* from TFP growth; reallocation added ~0; entry/exit added positively but not enough. Importantly, **giving LA5 other regions' industry mix barely changes its TFP growth** — the gap is within industries.

## Informality

- **Dual view (La Porta & Shleifer L035):** informal firms are small, unproductive and rarely graduate; development happens as formal firms displace them.
- **Voluntary/heterogeneous view (Maloney L064; Ulyssea L036):** much informality is a rational choice; informal firms are heterogeneous ("survival", "parasite", "exclusion" types); enforcement can raise TFP but cut employment.
- **Institutions-induced misallocation (Levy L033):** in Mexico, tax, social-insurance and labor rules tax productive formal salaried employment and subsidize informal and non-salaried arrangements. Informal firms are >90% of firms, ~55% of employment, ~50% less productive — and grew faster than formal firms after the reforms. Education and infrastructure are not the binding constraints (Mexico City has similar informality).
- **Missing middle? (Hsieh & Olken L037):** there are many tiny firms and few large ones, but not a missing-middle bump; size-threshold stories should be tested, not assumed.

## What this strand gives the project

1. A micro foundation for LAC's TFP stagnation: firms don't grow, good firms are taxed, bad firms survive, and within-firm productivity growth is weak.
2. Evidence that the relevant dualism is **inside** industries (formal vs. informal; large vs. micro), not only between them.
3. A policy-institution channel: the social contract (contributory social insurance for formal salaried workers, non-contributory programs for everyone else) shapes the job structure.

## Gaps

- Most misallocation estimates are static; they say little about *dynamic* losses (learning, capability accumulation) from having the workforce in micro-units.
- Little integration of misallocation with structural change: the two literatures use different units of analysis.
